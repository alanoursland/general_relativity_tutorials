# Bibliography

The literature the source paper engages with, grouped by role in its argument.
Entries give author/year and topic exactly as characterized in the paper — no
publication details beyond that are fabricated here. Each line notes what the
work contributed and where it's used in this book.

## Historical origin
- **Michell (1784).** The original dark-star argument: set Newtonian escape
  velocity equal to $c$ and solve, landing on $r=2GM/c^2$ — numerically the
  Schwarzschild radius, a century before GR, using Newtonian mechanics well
  outside its domain of validity. Used throughout
  [`HIST1`](../part7-michell-pg/hist1-michell-laplace.md) and the book's
  running "coincidence, explained, not vindicated" theme.
- **Laplace.** Independently reached the same escape-velocity conclusion as
  Michell. Discussed alongside Michell in
  [`HIST1`](../part7-michell-pg/hist1-michell-laplace.md).

## The "simple derivation" lineage
- **Lenz–Sommerfeld** (as reported by Sommerfeld, 1952). An early entry in the
  lineage of relativistic reinterpretations of Newtonian escape-velocity
  reasoning, aiming to reach Schwarzschild by a shortcut. Cataloged in the
  §7.1-style survey at [`SYN1`](../part8-synthesis/syn1-century-of-shortcuts.md).
- **Schiff (1960).** Another simple-derivation attempt in the same lineage.
  Cataloged at [`SYN1`](../part8-synthesis/syn1-century-of-shortcuts.md).
- **Tangherlini (1962).** Continues the lineage of Newtonian-motion
  reinterpretations aimed at Schwarzschild. Cataloged at
  [`SYN1`](../part8-synthesis/syn1-century-of-shortcuts.md).
- **Sacks & Ball (1968).** A further simple-derivation entry in the same
  tradition. Cataloged at [`SYN1`](../part8-synthesis/syn1-century-of-shortcuts.md).
- **Cuzinatto–Pimentel–Pompeia (2011).** A modern continuation of the same
  lineage of "simple derivations" of the Schwarzschild metric. Cataloged at
  [`SYN1`](../part8-synthesis/syn1-century-of-shortcuts.md).

## Counterexamples and no-go results
- **Rindler (1968, 1969).** Counterexamples showing that the "usual
  ingredients" invoked in simple derivations do not, by themselves, uniquely
  determine the metric — the negative-space fact this book's family
  construction turns into a positive, constructive statement of *what they do
  determine*. See [`CORE4`](../part3-building-the-family/core4-classification-theorem.md)
  and [`SYN1`](../part8-synthesis/syn1-century-of-shortcuts.md).
- **Gruber, Price, Matthews, Cordwell & Wagner (1988).** A systematic analysis
  of why the standard ingredients fail to uniquely determine the metric — the
  fullest statement of the no-go result the present paper answers
  constructively. See [`CORE4`](../part3-building-the-family/core4-classification-theorem.md).

## Modern reconstructions
- **Visser (2005).** Shows a Newtonian heuristic reproduces Schwarzschild in
  Painlevé–Gullstrand coordinates, but leaves the agreement unexplained — the
  gap this paper's PG chapter closes. See
  [`PG3`](../part7-michell-pg/pg3-newtonian-infall.md) and
  [`SYN1`](../part8-synthesis/syn1-century-of-shortcuts.md).
- **Czerniawski (2006).** A related exact-Newtonian-infall-in-flat-slice
  reconstruction, grouped with Visser (2005) in the paper's own survey. See
  [`SYN1`](../part8-synthesis/syn1-century-of-shortcuts.md).
- **Kassner (2015).** Derives a weak-field metric from an enlarged set of
  assumptions, leaving one nonlinear coefficient to be fixed by Mercury's
  perihelion data — the same role $\lambda$ plays here, with $\lambda=\beta-2$.
  See [`PPN6`](../part6-ppn-observation/ppn6-reading-constraints.md).
- **Kassner (2017).** Extends the same program (orbital and dust-ball
  reconstructions), supplying further physical/observational information
  beyond the base construction. Cataloged at
  [`SYN1`](../part8-synthesis/syn1-century-of-shortcuts.md).
- **Kassner (2019).** Selects Schwarzschild among Shuler's (2018) three
  candidate metrics via a standard relativistic generalization of Newton's
  law — a *selection* condition applied on top of a family that, in this
  paper, is constructed first and independently. See
  [`CORE5`](../part3-building-the-family/core5-distinguished-members.md) and
  [`SYN1`](../part8-synthesis/syn1-century-of-shortcuts.md).
- **Shuler (2018).** Presents Schwarzschild plus two alternative metrics in a
  common parameterized form $g_{tt}=-(1+n\,GM/(rc^2))^{-2/n}$; his
  $n=-2,-1,+1$ correspond to $\lambda=-1,-\tfrac12,+\tfrac12$ in this book's
  family — three named points on the same continuum. See
  [`CORE5`](../part3-building-the-family/core5-distinguished-members.md).

## Bootstrap / field-theoretic approaches
- **Deser (1970).** Field self-coupling / tree-graph reconstruction: demands
  full dynamical self-consistency of the gravitational field's own coupling to
  its own stress-energy, a stronger construction than static accounting that
  the paper treats as *not* itself a selection story within the $\lambda$-family
  but as the genuine source of the dynamical information static accounting
  lacks. See [`OUT1`](../part8-synthesis/out1-self-coupling-bootstrap.md).
- **Duff (1973).** A further self-coupling / bootstrap construction in the same
  spirit as Deser (1970). See
  [`OUT1`](../part8-synthesis/out1-self-coupling-bootstrap.md).
- **Casadio et al. (2018, 2021).** A related bootstrap using nonlinear Poisson
  self-interaction to construct an effective metric — explicitly not the same
  thing as the static local-energy assignment of Sec. 4 of the paper, and not
  itself a selection within the $\lambda$-family. See
  [`SYN1`](../part8-synthesis/syn1-century-of-shortcuts.md) and
  [`OUT1`](../part8-synthesis/out1-self-coupling-bootstrap.md).

## PPN formalism and solar-system tests
- **Will (2014).** The standard reference for the parametrized post-Newtonian
  (PPN) formalism this book adopts wholesale for $\gamma$ and $\beta$. See
  [`PPN1`](../part6-ppn-observation/ppn1-ppn-formalism.md).
- **Nordtvedt (1968).** Predicts the Nordtvedt effect — a possible violation of
  the strong equivalence principle by gravitational self-energy — supplying
  the physical motivation for asking whether binding energy "gravitates
  universally" in Part V. See [`ACC0`](../part5-energy-accounting/acc0-the-question.md).
- **Williams, Turyshev & Boggs (2004).** Lunar laser ranging plus Cassini
  tracking constraint on $\beta$: $\beta-1=(1.2\pm1.1)\times10^{-4}$, used as
  the headline solar-system bound, with the caveat that it assumes the
  restricted PPN relation $\eta_N=4\beta-\gamma-3$ this book's one-body
  exterior doesn't itself establish. See
  [`PPN6`](../part6-ppn-observation/ppn6-reading-constraints.md).
- **Fienga et al. (2022), INPOP20a.** A modern planetary-ephemeris bound,
  $|\beta-1|\lesssim7.2\times10^{-5}$ (a conservative acceptance bound), used
  alongside Williams–Turyshev–Boggs for the order-of-magnitude conclusion
  $|\lambda+1|\lesssim10^{-4}$. See
  [`PPN6`](../part6-ppn-observation/ppn6-reading-constraints.md).

## Coordinates
- **Painlevé (1921).** Co-originator of the Painlevé–Gullstrand time slicing
  used throughout Part VII to make Schwarzschild's spatial slices flat. See
  [`PG1`](../part7-michell-pg/pg1-painleve-gullstrand.md).
- **Gullstrand (1922).** Co-originator of the same coordinate system,
  independently. See [`PG1`](../part7-michell-pg/pg1-painleve-gullstrand.md).
- **Hamilton & Lisle (2008).** Source of the "river model" language for the PG
  velocity $v(r)$ — a representation of a coordinate shift, explicitly not a
  material flow. See [`PG1`](../part7-michell-pg/pg1-painleve-gullstrand.md).
- **Jacobson (2007).** Gives a general condition under which the reciprocal
  relation $g_{tt}g_{rr}=-1$ (assumption I3) can be justified, rather than
  simply assumed. See [`CORE0`](../part3-building-the-family/core0-four-assumptions.md).
